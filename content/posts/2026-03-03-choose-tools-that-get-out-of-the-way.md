There is always another editor, task manager, theme, or note-taking app to try. Exploring tools can be fun, but it can also become a way to delay the work the tool was supposed to help with.

Before switching, I like to ask three questions:

1. What specific friction am I trying to remove?
2. Does the new tool solve that friction in a way I can explain?
3. Will moving my existing work cost more than the improvement is worth?

If the answer to the first question is vague, I wait. A tool should solve a real problem that has shown up more than once.

## Make a small trial

When a tool still looks promising, try it on one small task. Keep the trial short and define what success looks like beforehand. For example: “Can I find yesterday’s notes faster?” is more useful than “Does this app feel better?”

The best setup is often the one that lets you return to the work quickly. It does not have to be the most customizable one.
