When learning something new, it is easy to collect tabs, bookmarks, and half-finished notes. A small learning log gives those fragments a place to land.

It does not need to be a polished journal. A few lines are enough:

- **Today I tried:** the thing I worked on.
- **I understand now:** one idea that became clearer.
- **I am still unsure about:** the next question to investigate.
- **Next time:** one small action to restart with.

The last line is especially useful. It turns returning to a topic into a simple next step instead of another planning session.

## Keep the format easy

Use plain Markdown, a text file, or whatever tool you already open every day. The goal is to reduce the effort of recording an idea. If writing the note takes longer than the learning session, the format is probably too elaborate.

Over time, these notes become a personal map of how your understanding changed. They also make it easier to explain an idea in your own words, which is a good test of whether it has really settled in.
