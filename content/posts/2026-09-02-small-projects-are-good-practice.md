A small project still has a beginning, a middle, and an ending. You choose a scope, make decisions, run into a problem, and decide what “finished” means. That makes it a useful practice space.

For example, a tiny reading list can teach you about:

- organizing data;
- designing a simple interface;
- handling empty and invalid input;
- writing a short setup guide;
- publishing a working result.

The project does not need accounts, recommendations, or a mobile app on the first day. A single page that saves a few books may be enough to complete the full loop.

## Define the finish line early

Write down the smallest version that would be useful. Put extra ideas in a separate list and return to them only after the first version works. Finishing gives you something concrete to review and improve; an expanding plan does not.
